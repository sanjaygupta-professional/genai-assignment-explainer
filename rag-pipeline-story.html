Chapter 1

Navigating Disability Benefits in India

India recognizes 21 types of disabilities under the RPwD Act 2016. Hundreds of government schemes exist across central and state levels — each with different eligibility criteria for disability type, age, income, and geography. For a family with a child who has cerebral palsy, finding the right schemes means navigating mountains of bureaucratic PDFs in languages they may not speak fluently.

A mother in Karnataka asks: "My daughter is 12 with locomotor disability. Our income is Rs 1.2 lakh/year. Which schemes can she get?" Today, answering this requires reading 6+ government PDFs, understanding eligibility matrices, and cross-referencing criteria manually. Most families give up.

Why Standard RAG Falls Short

A pure RAG system embeds document chunks and searches by semantic similarity. It works for open-ended questions but fails on structured eligibility queries with multiple conjunctive filters:

Vector Search Handles Well

  • "What are common disability schemes?"
  • "How does Niramaya insurance work?"
  • "Explain the UDID card process"

Vector Search Struggles With

  • "Schemes for locomotor, age 12, Karnataka, income < 1.5L"
  • "Schemes covering both autism and cerebral palsy"
  • "All schemes needing only Aadhaar + disability cert"
Chapter 2

The Hybrid Approach

The core insight: use two complementary retrieval paths, each doing what it's best at, then merge the results.

Neither path alone is sufficient. Vectors can't do conjunctive filtering ("locomotor AND age 12 AND Karnataka AND income < 1.5L"). Graphs can't do semantic similarity ("tell me about schemes for children who can't walk"). Together they cover both modes of information seeking.

Chapter 5

The Knowledge Graph

The KG encodes structured eligibility metadata extracted from the government PDFs. It answers what vector search cannot: "Which schemes cover locomotor disability, are available in Karnataka, for age 12, with income below Rs 1.5 lakh?"

Example Cypher Query

-- Find schemes for a 12-year-old with locomotor disability in Karnataka MATCH (s:Scheme)-[:COVERS]->(d:DisabilityCategory), (s)-[:AVAILABLE_IN]->(st:State), (s)-[:FOR_AGE_GROUP]->(ag:AgeGroup) WHERE d.name CONTAINS 'locomotor' AND (st.name CONTAINS 'Karnataka' OR st.id = 'all_india') AND ag.min_age <= 12 AND ag.max_age >= 12 RETURN s.name, s.benefit_type, s.benefit_value_inr
My daughter is 12 years old with locomotor disability. We live in Karnataka and our family income is Rs 1.2 lakh per year.
ADIP Scheme Pre-Matric Scholarship DDRS SIPDA
4 schemes matched — all 4 criteria (disability + age + state + income) resolved via single Cypher query

Roadmap: From PoC to Production

Completed
Working PoC with hybrid retrieval
6 documents, 8 schemes in KG, text-mode embeddings, CLI + REPL, 5 verified demo queries.
Next
PDF-native embeddings via Gemini Embedding 2
Switch from text-mode to raw PDF byte embedding. Preserves layout, tables, and visual structure.
Enhancement
Expand KG with 200+ state-level schemes
Scrape state disability department portals for structured metadata. Scale KG to ~500 schemes across all states.
Enhancement
WhatsApp/Telegram bot for real families
Voice-to-text input for accessibility. Inline eligibility forms that auto-populate KG query parameters.
Production
Migrate to Neo4j Aura + Qdrant Cloud
Neo4j Aura free tier (200K nodes). Qdrant Cloud managed hosting. Auth, rate limiting, monitoring.
Research
Agentic document update pipeline
Agent monitors gov.in feeds, detects scheme changes, re-embeds affected chunks, updates KG nodes automatically.
The hybrid architecture was designed for extensibility. Adding a new scheme requires only: (1) download the PDF and run ingest.py for vector embeddings, (2) add structured metadata to the KG. The retrieval and generation layers need zero changes.
