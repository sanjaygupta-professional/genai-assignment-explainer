The Playbook

Eight steps from blank page to polished submission. Follow this sequence and you will naturally hit every rubric criterion.

  1. 1

    Pick a Problem Worth Solving

    Choose a business problem where Gen AI creates a defensible advantage — not just automation. The rubric rewards “well-defined and complex” problems. Think: unstructured data, domain expertise bottleneck, high-volume decisions. Avoid toy problems.

    Pick problems where humans currently spend 30+ minutes per decision using scattered information. That's where RAG + LLM creates the most compelling before/after story.
  2. 2

    Write Vision & Mission Statements

    Vision: Where the world is going (aspirational, 5-year horizon). Mission: What your product does to get there (concrete, measurable). These anchor your entire pitch.

    Example — Legal Document Intelligence
    Vision: A world where legal professionals spend their time on strategy and advocacy, not document review.
    Mission: Build an AI-powered document analysis platform that reduces contract review time by 80% while flagging critical risks with 95%+ recall.
  3. 3

    Design the Gen AI Solution

    This is 20% of your grade (10 points). Your solution must clearly use Gen AI techniques from the course: LLMs, prompt engineering, RAG, fine-tuning, embeddings, agents. Name specific models and tools.

    The rubric says: “method of estimating the success of the approach is also clearly stated.” This means you MUST include evaluation metrics — not just architecture. How do you measure if it works?
  4. 4

    Build the Product Roadmap

    Another 10 points. Show a phased plan from MVP to full product. The rubric expects “short-term to 3 years” and “technically correct.”

    PhaseTimelineWhat to Include
    MVP0-3 monthsCore RAG pipeline, basic UI, 1 use case, internal testing
    Beta3-6 monthsMulti-use-case support, feedback loop, pilot users
    v1.06-12 monthsProduction deployment, integrations, analytics dashboard
    Scale1-3 yearsMulti-tenant, additional domains, agentic workflows, marketplace
  5. 5

    ROI Analysis

    Yet another 10 points. Cover both expense and revenue sides. The grader wants to see you can think about this as a business, not just a technology exercise.

    Generic statements like “AI saves time” score poorly. Quantify everything: “Contract review currently takes 4 hours × $200/hr paralegal cost. Our tool reduces this to 45 minutes, saving $650 per contract. At 200 contracts/month, annual savings = $1.56M.”
  6. 6

    Build the 10-Slide Elevator Pitch

    This is your “Quality of Report and Presentation” criterion (10 points). Ten slides max — make every one count.

    Recommended Slide Structure
    1. Problem & Market Size   2. Vision & Mission   3. Solution Overview   4. Gen AI Architecture   5. Demo / Proof of Concept   6. Product Roadmap   7. Business Model & ROI   8. Competitive Landscape   9. Team & Unique Aspects   10. Ask / Next Steps
  7. 7

    Highlight Unique Aspects

    The final 5 points reward uniqueness. The rubric explicitly mentions three dimensions: patent potential, publishable quality, and VC-presentable. You don't need all three — but aim for at least one.

    Frame your unique angle explicitly: “Our approach to [specific technique] could be filed as a provisional patent because...” or “This methodology section could be adapted into a conference paper for [venue].” Don't leave the grader guessing.
  8. 8

    Polish & Rehearse

    Oral presentations happen in Sessions 14-15. The quality of your presentation is scored — not just the written report. Rehearse as a group. Time your pitch to 10 minutes or less.

Problem Idea Arena

You can choose your own problem or build on the 10 jobs from Assignment 1. Here are high-impact problem spaces that naturally suit Gen AI solutions:

ROI Analysis Framework

Structure your ROI as a two-sided balance sheet. The rubric says “covering expenses and revenue aspects.”

Costs (Year 1)

  • LLM API costs (per-token pricing)
  • Vector DB hosting (Pinecone/Weaviate)
  • Embedding computation
  • Development team (FTEs)
  • Cloud infrastructure (compute, storage)
  • KB curation & maintenance
  • Security & compliance

Value & Revenue

  • Time saved × hourly labor cost
  • Error reduction × cost per error
  • Throughput increase (more decisions/day)
  • Subscription revenue (SaaS model)
  • Reduced onboarding time for new hires
  • Competitive advantage / market share
  • Data-driven insights (new revenue stream)

Scoring Maximizer

Check every item before submitting. Each maps directly to rubric language.

Problem Definition 5 points
  • Problem is specific and clearly scoped (not vague “improve efficiency”)
  • Explained why this problem is suitable for Gen AI specifically
  • Defined clear objectives with measurable success criteria
  • Described the current state (how is this done today, what's the pain)
  • Estimated market size or organizational impact
Gen AI Solution Design 10 points
  • Named specific Gen AI techniques used (RAG, fine-tuning, agents, etc.)
  • Chose specific tools and models (not “an LLM” but “GPT-4o-mini via Azure OpenAI”)
  • Described the full pipeline: data ingestion → processing → inference → output
  • Included evaluation methodology with specific metrics
  • Explained why Gen AI vs traditional ML/rule-based approaches
  • Addressed data requirements (what data, how much, quality needs)
Product Roadmap 10 points
  • Phased timeline from MVP to 3-year vision
  • Each phase has specific deliverables (not vague milestones)
  • Technical architecture is correct and feasible
  • Addressed scaling strategy (users, data volume, features)
  • Included risk mitigation for each phase
  • Aligned with vision/mission statements
ROI Analysis 10 points
  • Quantified costs (development, infrastructure, ongoing operations)
  • Quantified revenue or value (time saved, error reduction, new revenue)
  • Multi-year projection (at least 3 years)
  • Break-even analysis (when does investment pay off?)
  • Compared ROI to current state / alternative solutions
  • Stated assumptions clearly
Report & Presentation Quality 10 points
  • Elevator pitch is exactly 10 slides (not more)
  • Slides are visual, not walls of text
  • Consistent design theme across all slides
  • Written report is well-structured with clear sections
  • Figures and diagrams are labeled and referenced
  • Group practiced the oral presentation together
Unique Aspects 5 points
  • Explicitly stated what makes this unique/novel
  • Addressed at least one: patent potential, publishable, VC-presentable
  • Described competitive differentiation (what's new vs existing solutions)
  • If applicable: described IP or data moat
Structure your report sections to mirror the 6 rubric criteria — Problem Definition, Solution Design, Product Roadmap, ROI Analysis, Unique Aspects. Make the grader's job easy. When they look for “ROI Analysis,” there should be a section titled exactly that.

Assignment 1 vs Group Assignment

These are fundamentally different exercises. Don't reuse your Assignment 1 without rethinking the framing.

DimensionAssignment 1 (25 marks)Group Assignment (50 marks)
ScopeOne pre-defined task for one jobAny business problem (your choice)
FocusRAG architecture designFull product: vision → roadmap → ROI
Output7+3 page written noteWritten report + 10-slide pitch + oral presentation
EvaluationTechnical correctnessBusiness viability + technical design + uniqueness
TeamIndividualGroup collaboration
Unique angleNot scored5 points for patent/publishable/VC potential
You can build on your Assignment 1 job — but the group assignment demands you go far beyond a RAG pipeline. You need business context (market, competition, revenue model), a roadmap, and ROI. Think of Assignment 1 as the technical kernel inside a much larger business wrapper.
