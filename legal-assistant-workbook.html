Build Approach — Why RAG?

Q3 — Frames Criterion 2
Legal Domain Context
Case law databases update continuously as new decisions are filed. Search strategies evolve as new cases set precedent. A static model would become outdated within weeks of new rulings.
Rubric Reminder
  • Q3 asks you to compare at least 3 approaches: from-scratch training, fine-tuning, pure prompting, and RAG. You must argue why RAG wins for your specific job.
  • Use concrete reasons tied to your KB — e.g., "new cases filed weekly make the tuning stale fast" is stronger than "RAG is flexible."
  • A comparison table earns clear marks for structure.
Q3: Approach Comparison Table
ApproachFeasibility for Legal KBKey Limitation
Train from ScratchNot feasibleKB is ~500 search session logs. No justification for training a model from scratch on this tiny corpus.
Fine-TuningPartially feasibleCould fine-tune on search logs, but new cases filed weekly make the tuning stale fast. Re-tuning on each update is expensive and slow.
Pure PromptingPartially feasibleContext window can hold a few search logs at a time, but can't scale to hundreds of sessions. Loses the ability to filter by jurisdiction or case_type.
RAG (chosen)OptimalDynamically retrieves relevant search sessions at query time. New logs added by re-indexing only. Metadata filtering enables jurisdiction-specific retrieval. Full citation provenance to source sessions.
For the legal assistant context, the killer argument for RAG is metadata-filtered retrieval: lawyers need results filtered by jurisdiction, case_type, and success_rating — capabilities that pure prompting and fine-tuning simply cannot provide. RAG's vector store metadata filters make this natural.

RAG Architecture Design

Criterion 2 5 marks
Legal Domain Context
Legal search has a unique requirement: precision matters more than recall. A lawyer needs the right cases, not all vaguely related cases. This shapes our retrieval strategy toward hybrid search (BM25 + dense) and re-ranking.
Rubric Reminder
  • Q4 requires a full RAG pipeline description: ingestion, chunking, embedding, vector store, retrieval, augmentation, generation, and output.
  • Name your chosen tools/models at each stage (e.g., "OpenAI text-embedding-3-small for embeddings, Pinecone for vector store").
  • Include your evaluation methodology: how will you measure if the RAG system is working? Mention at least 2 metrics.
  • A flow diagram or numbered pipeline steps earns clarity marks.
Q4: Full RAG Pipeline — 8-Stage Architecture

Stage 1 — Document Ingestion

Source formats: text logs (structured JSON/CSV), PDF case briefs, internal strategy guides (DOCX). Tools: custom JSON parser for session logs, pypdf2 for PDF guides, python-docx for DOCX. Output: structured text with metadata.

Stage 2 — Legal-Aware Chunking

Strategy: session logs chunked as whole entries (1–3 chunks per session, ~300–500 tokens). Strategy guides chunked by section heading. searcher_notes kept as atomic chunks (never split mid-strategy). Rationale: search strategies lose meaning when split across chunks.

Stage 3 — Embedding

Model: text-embedding-3-small (OpenAI, 1536 dimensions). Each chunk embedded. Cost: ~$0.02/million tokens. Legal terminology captured well by general-purpose embeddings since search logs use natural language.

Stage 4 — Vector Store

Platform: Pinecone (or Chroma for local). Index: cosine similarity. Metadata filters: case_type, jurisdiction, success_rating, filing_context, date_searched. Namespace per practice area for scoped retrieval.

Stage 5 — Hybrid Retrieval (BM25 + Dense)

Query embedded with same model. Hybrid search: BM25 for exact legal terms (case names, statutes) + dense for semantic similarity. Top-k=8 from each, merged and deduplicated. Rationale: legal search terms are precise (statute numbers, case names) where keyword matching outperforms pure semantic search.

Stage 6 — Context Augmentation

Retrieved chunks formatted with source metadata: [Source: Session sess_2024_0342, Case Type: breach_of_contract, Jurisdiction: CA, Success: 4/5]. System prompt instructs model to cite session IDs. Total context budget: ~4,000 tokens.

Stage 7 — Generation

LLM: gpt-4o via Azure. Temperature: 0.2 (factual retrieval task). Max tokens: 600. Model instructed to suggest search strategies based on retrieved sessions, cite source sessions, and refuse legal advice requests.

Stage 8 — Output & Evaluation

Response includes session citations. Evaluation: (a) Faithfulness — do suggested search terms match retrieved session logs? (b) Relevance — does the response address the specific case type and jurisdiction? (c) Usefulness — would a lawyer find the suggested search strategy actionable? Tools: manual evaluation with practicing attorneys + automated RAGAS metrics.

System Prompt (Reference Version)
# System Prompt: Legal Case Search Assistant You are a legal research search assistant that helps lawyers find related cases by leveraging past search session logs. Your role: - Help lawyers develop search strategies for finding related cases, based on ONLY the retrieved search session logs provided below. - Always cite the source session ID for every recommendation (format: [Source: Session sess_XXXX_XXXX]). - Suggest search terms, case names, and search strategies that were successful in similar past searches. - If the retrieved context does not contain relevant search sessions, say: "I don't have matching search session logs for this case type/jurisdiction. Consider consulting your firm's research librarian." Critical boundary: - You are a SEARCH STRATEGY assistant, NOT a legal advisor. - NEVER provide legal opinions, case merit assessments, or filing recommendations. - If asked for legal advice, respond: "I can help you find related cases, but legal analysis and advice should come from a qualified attorney. Would you like me to suggest search strategies instead?" - Do not assess whether found cases support or undermine a legal argument. Format: - Organize suggestions by: (1) Recommended search terms, (2) Similar cases found in past sessions, (3) Strategy notes from experienced searchers. - Include the success_rating from source sessions so lawyers can prioritize proven strategies. - Keep responses under 500 words unless the query requires multi-jurisdiction comparison. Context passages will be injected below by the system.
Few-Shot Examples (2 pairs)
Few-Shot Example 1 — Successful Search Strategy
Query: "I need to find cases related to a breach of contract involving a SaaS agreement in California."

Expected Response: "Based on past search sessions for similar cases, here are recommended strategies: Search Terms: 'breach of contract SaaS', 'software licensing agreement breach California', 'cloud services contract dispute' [Source: Session sess_2024_0342, Success: 4/5]. Similar Cases Found: Past searchers found Oracle v. Google (593 U.S. 1) and Versata Software v. SAP relevant for software contract disputes [Source: Session sess_2024_0342]. Strategy Notes: 'Start with broad contract terms, then narrow by industry (software/SaaS) and state. California has strong precedent in tech contract disputes.' [Source: Session sess_2024_0342, searcher_notes]."
Few-Shot Example 2 — Legal Advice Refusal
Query: "Should I file this case in federal or state court?"

Expected Response: "I can help you find related cases, but legal analysis and advice — including jurisdictional filing decisions — should come from a qualified attorney. Would you like me to suggest search strategies for finding cases that were filed in different jurisdictions for similar case types instead?"
RAG Optimization Strategies

Strategy 1: Jurisdiction Pre-Filtering

Before embedding the query, detect jurisdiction mentions (state names, circuit numbers, "federal") and apply a metadata filter to narrow retrieval to matching sessions. Prevents California searches from returning New York results where legal standards differ significantly.

Strategy 2: Legal Term Expansion

Use a lightweight LLM call to expand legal search queries with related terms. "breach of contract" → also search "contract violation", "material breach", "anticipatory breach". Legal terminology has many synonyms that pure embedding similarity might miss.

Strategy 3: Success-Weighted Re-Ranking

After initial retrieval, boost chunks from sessions with success_rating ≥ 4. A search strategy that worked (high rating) is more valuable than one that didn't. Weight formula: score × (0.5 + 0.1 × success_rating).

Rubric Reminder

  • Each limitation needs a paired mitigation strategy — not just a problem list.
  • Domain-specific risks for legal: liability if search suggestions lead to missed cases, attorney-client privilege in search logs, case law currency, jurisdictional accuracy, hallucinated citations.
  • Be specific about consequences: "hallucinated case citation" in a legal brief could result in court sanctions.

Suggested Tryout Protocol

Step 2: Open ChatGPT or Azure OpenAI
